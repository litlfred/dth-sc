A

Actor (Conformance Model) candidate

An Actor is an abstract, well-bounded role in the health information ecosystem that is defined entirely by the information-processing responsibilities it assumes and the interactions it participates in. An Actor is neither a software product nor an organisational unit.

Defined in RA §3.7.2 (library/who-dpi-h-reference-architecture-draft-v1/sections/sec-050-372-the-model.md, p. 111–116). Substantive distinction from the platform's `actor` concept in folio-assistant-core/glossary/cat-harness.glossary.json ("A concrete participant, human, agentic or mechanical, that persists across every process and takes on roles"): the RA's conformance-model Actor is an abstract information-processing role realised by concrete systems, closer to IHE's Actor or the platform's Role (smart-ra#133 S1).

Adverse Events Following Immunization AEFI candidate

Adverse effects following immunisation

Recorded per smart-ra#142 item 4: the PC handbook abbreviations list (who-iris/library/9789240116191-eng/sections/page-006.md, p. 6) expands AEFI as "Adverse effects following immunisation", whereas standard WHO immunization terminology expands AEFI as "Adverse events following immunization" (an 'event' is any untoward medical occurrence following immunization and does not presuppose a causal 'effect'). Both expansions are recorded as candidates; neither is silently altered.

Business Process candidate

A set of activities and tasks performed in coordination to achieve a specified business objective, or to deliver value in a specific way to a health service user. In the context of digitizing care pathways, this could include “health service user registration” and “referrals”, for example (see section 3.3).

The systematic application of information and communications technologies, computer science, and data to support informed decision-making by individuals, the health workforce, and health systems, to strengthen resilience to disease and improve health and wellness.

All are recorded as candidates; none is chosen.

The process of digitally optimizing and driving systems-level changes by leveraging innovation, analytics and feedback mechanisms to improve person-centred health systems. Digital technologies are used to fundamentally change and improve how health services are delivered and accessed across all health programme areas (12,14,15).
