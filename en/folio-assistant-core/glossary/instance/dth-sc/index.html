C

Centralized Traceability Model candidate

A traceability model that involves a single database or repository for storing all traceability data.

The handbook's sentence (PDF 54), with 'A traceability model' put in front so it reads as a definition. Open: not adopted.

Candidate definitions (2)
  1. Traceability (Glossary of the Digital Transformation Handbook for Health Supply Chain Architecture)

    The ability to trace something. In some cases, it is interpreted as the ability to verify the history, location or application of an item by means of documented identification.

    Handbook definition, quoted. Open: not adopted, nothing chosen between alternatives. The handbook gives a second definition in Chapter 4 (PDF 54), citing GS1: the ability 'to see the movement of health products across the supply chain'. Both are kept as alternatives; neither is chosen (litlfred/smart-ra#140). dth-pc and dth-sfp link to this term.

  2. Interoperability (Glossary of the Reference Architecture and Guidance for DPI-H)

    Interoperability is the standards-based capability of heterogeneous information systems to communicate, exchange data, and use the exchanged information to execute functions effectively.

    The RA draft's own glossary wording, verbatim (folio/dpi-h-ra/glossary/t-glossary-lead-a31853.md). Substantive and internal alternatives across the DTH corpus (smart-ra#133 S1, smart-ra#139, smart-ra#140, smart-ra#142): (1) PHC handbook glossary (who-iris/library/9789240093362-eng/sections/sec-002-glossary.md, p. 9), PC handbook glossary (who-iris/library/9789240116191-eng/sections/page-008.md, p. 8), and SFP handbook glossary (who-iris/library/9789240120747-eng/sections/page-010.md, p. 10): "Ability of different applications to access, exchange, integrate and use data in a coordinated and consistent manner through the use of shared application interfaces, value sets, concepts and standards, within and across organizational, regional and national boundaries, to provide timely and seamless portability of information and optimize health outcomes." (2) SC handbook glossary (who-iris/library/9789240101197-eng/sections/page-008.md, p. 8, citing DIIG): "The ability of different applications to access, exchange, integrate and use data in a coordinated and consistent manner, through the use of shared application interfaces, value sets, concepts and standards within and across organizational, regional and national boundaries. In the health context, this provides timely, safe and seamless portability of information to help ensure good health outcomes (1)." (3) PHC handbook §4.4 body (who-iris/library/9789240093362-eng/sections/sec-023-44-design-system-architecture.md, §4.4, pp. 56-60): "Interoperability is the ability of different applications to access, exchange, integrate and use data in a coordinated manner through the use of shared application interfaces and standards, which should be defined by the EA and independent of a particular solution’s system architecture (22)." (4) PHC handbook §4.4.2 body (who-iris/library/9789240093362-eng/sections/sec-023-44-design-system-architecture.md, pp. 56-60): "Interoperability is the ability of different applications to access, exchange, integrate and use data in a coordinated manner through the use of shared application interfaces and data representation standards, within and across organizational, regional and national boundaries, to provide timely and seamless portability of information and optimize health outcomes." All are recorded as candidates; none is chosen.

Digital Health Supply Chain DHSC candidate

The knowledge and practice of adopting digital technologies to improve health supply chains and enable the delivery of quality-assured health products to individuals when and where they need them.

Handbook definition, quoted. Open: not adopted, nothing chosen between alternatives. Defined in the Introduction (PDF 12), not in the handbook's glossary.

Candidate definitions (2)
  1. Health Digital Public Infrastructure (Glossary of the Reference Architecture and Guidance for DPI-H)

    Health Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI-H) refers to digital resources that are built as a shared means for fulfilling multiple health agency functions.

    The RA draft's own glossary wording, verbatim (folio/dpi-h-ra/glossary/t-glossary-lead-a31853.md). Wording alternative in RA §1.1.2 body (library/who-dpi-h-reference-architecture-draft-v1/sections/sec-002-112-understanding-digital-public-infrastructure.md), which adds a second defining sentence: "Health Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI-H) refers to digital resources that are built as a shared means for fulfilling multiple health agency functions. These digital resources for health — encompassing both systems and specifications — leverage foundational DPI, are interoperable, reusable, publicly governed and built on open standards." Both are recorded as candidates; neither is chosen (smart-ra#133 S1).

  2. Enterprise Architecture (Glossary of the Reference Architecture and Guidance for DPI-H)

    The organising logic for business processes and IT infrastructure reflecting the integration and standardisation requirements of the organisation's operating model.

    The RA draft's own glossary wording, verbatim (folio/dpi-h-ra/glossary/t-glossary-lead-a31853.md); the draft marks this entry [Under review]. Substantive alternatives across the DTH corpus (smart-ra#133 S1, smart-ra#139, smart-ra#140): (1) PHC handbook glossary (who-iris/library/9789240093362-eng/sections/sec-002-glossary.md, p. 9, citing DIIG): "A blueprint of business processes, data, systems and technologies used to help planners, software developers and managers design increasingly complex systems to support the workflow and roles of people in a large enterprise, such as a health system (11)." (2) SC handbook glossary (who-iris/library/9789240101197-eng/sections/page-008.md, p. 8, citing DIIG): "A blueprint of business processes, data, systems and technologies that helps planners, software developers and managers design increasingly complex systems to support the workflow and roles of people in a large enterprise such as a health system (1)." (3) SFP handbook glossary (who-iris/library/9789240120747-eng/sections/page-009.md, p. 9, citing DIIG): "A blueprint of business processes, data, systems and technologies used to help implementers design increasingly complex systems to support the workflow and roles of people in a large enterprise, such as a health system (3)." All four are recorded as candidates; none is chosen.

Digital Health Supply Chain Intervention candidate

An intervention that involves the use of digital technologies to improve the management and operation of health supply chains. It aims to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of health supply chain processes such as planning, procurement, distribution and warehouse management.

Handbook definition, quoted. Open: not adopted, nothing chosen between alternatives.

Candidate definitions (2)
  1. Capability (Glossary of the Reference Architecture and Guidance for DPI-H)

    A high-level ability the health system possesses or needs in order to achieve its goals. It expresses what the health system is able to do, independently of how that ability is implemented.

    The RA draft's own glossary wording, verbatim (folio/dpi-h-ra/glossary/t-glossary-lead-a31853.md). Wording alternative in RA Table 3.1 (library/who-dpi-h-reference-architecture-draft-v1/sections/sec-026-31-the-architecture-description-approach.md): "An ability that an organisation, person, or system possesses — expressed in terms of what can be done, not how or by whom." Both are recorded as candidates; neither is chosen (smart-ra#133 S1).

  2. Application Component (Glossary of the Reference Architecture and Guidance for DPI-H)

    A deployable, modular piece of software that provides a defined set of functionality and holds the data it manages, exposing both through services.

    The RA draft's own wording, verbatim. A candidate until the RA editors adopt it through the public review.
